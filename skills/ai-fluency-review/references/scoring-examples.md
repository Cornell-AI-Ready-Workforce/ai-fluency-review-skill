# Rating examples

Use these examples to calibrate evidence, not to count keywords.

## Description

- A clear goal in one short task can support Emerging or Usually observed, depending on completeness. It cannot alone support consistency across contexts.
- Goal, audience, constraints, acceptance criteria, and risk boundaries repeated in two distinct contexts can support Consistent across contexts.

## Delegation

- Asking AI to “handle everything” without review points is rarely observable delegation of responsibility.
- Explicit AI tasks, retained human decisions, approval points, and escalation rules across several contexts can support Consistent and reusable.

## Discernment

- A caught contradiction is positive evidence when the person notices and acts on it.
- Opening a cited source is interaction only. Inspecting the source, identifying a mismatch, and revising the work supports discernment.

## Diligence

- Saying “I checked it” without visible verification is reported, not visible.
- Source checks, tests, privacy handling, and a durable completion record support diligence when attributable to the person.

## Adaptive Flexibility

- Fixing one answer after feedback does not establish change over time.
- Friction followed by a changed workflow, verification that the change worked, and reuse on a later task can support a longitudinal conclusion.

## Sparse or unequal evidence

- Use `null` when the opportunity to observe the behavior was absent or attribution is ambiguous.
- Do not lower a rating merely because a source was unavailable.
- Do not compare periods with materially different task opportunities as though they were equivalent.
